Ripples into Silence

Lives That Vanished Within Sight of Lampedusa

The central Mediterranean has been a crossing point and a graveyard for years.
Each journey begins with hope, but not all end in safety.

Near the island of Lampedusa, the sea carries thousands of untold stories.
In 2013, one tragedy alone claimed over 360 lives just offshore.

More than a decade has passed since that day.
Yet boats still arrive — and others still vanish.

Some disappear far from help.
Others make it close, within sight of land, before fading like ripples on the sea.

What can these scattered traces tell us?
And what stories lie just beneath the surface?

Only 50 Kilometers Away

From 2014 to 2024, 94 incidents were recorded in the waters around Lampedusa. 703 people died or went missing. Every one of them had come within fifty kilometers of land.

Why Lampedusa?

Lampedusa may be small, but it is Italy's southernmost point—a remote island closer to Africa than to the European mainland. Just 130 kilometers from Tunisia, it lies at the very edge of Europe's southern border. For anyone crossing the central Mediterranean, it is usually the first land within reach. That is why the route exists.

The Tunisian Corridor: A Deadly Shortcut

The route from Sfax, Tunisia to Lampedusa is used so heavily that it now has a name: the Tunisian Corridor. It is one of the shortest crossings in the central Mediterranean, and one of the deadliest. Over the past decade this stretch of water has taken hundreds of lives. Not because of distance, but because of the lack of rescue, infrastructure, and safe alternatives. This is not one disaster. It is a record that stays quiet for years and then does not. What follows is the toll it has taken, from 2014 to 2024.

11 Years

Every disc is the same fifty kilometers of water, read the same way as the sequence above: how far out a mark sits is how far out the incident was, and its size is how many people were lost. For eight of these years the record holds a handful of incidents. Then 2022 holds seventeen, 2023 holds thirty-nine, and 2024 holds nineteen that killed more people than any year before it.

In 2024

45,997 people reached Lampedusa in 2024, on 1,095 boats, according to the Italian Red Cross, which runs the reception center on the island. In the same water that year, 206 did not.

46,203 people

Everyone who set out for Lampedusa in 2024 and reached this water. One dot is one person.

On clear days, Lampedusa is visible from far out at sea.
A silhouette. A hope.

They could see the island.
They could see Europe.
But seeing is not reaching.

If a single plane crash killed 200 people, we would mourn. We would change laws.

But when 206 people die across 19 migrant boats, while 45,997 others safely step onto the island, we tell ourselves — maybe that number's not so high.
After all, most made it.

And yet,
206 didn't.
